# Newsletter Structure

The newsletter should contain a small number of permanent sections and two required weekly content features.

The goal is consistency without repetition.

Every issue must include exactly one practical Homeowner / Seasonal Home Tip and exactly one Local Community Event. The event must have a direct reliable source and must occur after the Wednesday issue date. These two required features replace the former four-week rotating feature schedule.

### 2. Danny's Note

Include a short personal opening from Danny every week.

Target length:

**2 to 4 short sentences**

Purpose:

* Make the newsletter feel personal
* Introduce the week naturally
* Mention something seasonal, local, community-oriented, or relevant when appropriate

### 3. Gwinnett & Beyond Market Snapshot

Include a short real estate market snapshot every week.

Primary emphasis:

* Gwinnett County
* Nearby counties when useful and when reliable data is available

The section should explain what the numbers mean for ordinary homeowners, buyers, and sellers.

Do not overwhelm readers with statistics.

Prefer a few useful numbers and a short plain-English explanation.

Possible data points include:

* New listings
* Active inventory
* Pending sales
* Closed sales
* Median or average sales price
* Days on market
* Months of supply
* List-to-sale ratio
* Relevant week-over-week or year-over-year movement

Do not fabricate market statistics.

Use the latest available reliable data.

If current data cannot be verified, do not invent numbers.

# Rotating Content System

Each newsletter should normally contain:

**Two rotating feature sections**

Do not attempt to include every rotating category every week.

The purpose of the rotation is to make the newsletter feel fresh, local, useful, and enjoyable.

No editable editorial section may duplicate wording from a previous newsletter. Danny's Note, Market Snapshot commentary, rotating-feature titles and copy, Cartoon Corner image/caption/closing, preview text, and Referral/Share copy must be freshly written for each issue. Permanent structural and compliance elements, required contact details, URLs, and button labels are exempt because they must remain consistent.

Verification must fail when an editorial title, paragraph, list item, caption, closing line, or complete section exactly repeats content from any archived issue.

Real estate should remain present but should not dominate the newsletter.

# Automation Workflow

Every Sunday, the system should pre-stage the next Wednesday newsletter. Every Monday, it should finalize and email the verified HTML only to Danny.

The automated preparation process should:

1. Determine the next Wednesday issue date.
2. Determine the correct rotating-feature week.
3. Research current market information.
4. Research and verify the rotating local content.
5. Draft Danny's personal note.
6. Generate, host, and insert the fresh Cartoon Corner image.
7. Generate preview text.
8. Populate newsletter-data.json.
9. Archive the dated issue in issues/YYYY-MM-DD.json.
10. Generate HTML with generate.js.
11. Verify dist/newsletter.html.
12. Check links and required sections.
13. Check that outdated event information is not included.
14. Check that no unsupported market claims were created.
15. Check compliance placeholders.
16. Email the Command-ready HTML only to Danny.

The system may alert Danny if a required element cannot be verified rather than inventing information.

A Sunday GitHub Actions workflow performs steps 1-7 before Monday and commits the dated draft and cartoon asset so the separate Monday runner can load them. It may email only Danny a needs-attention alert after an unsuccessful final pre-stage. Events must come from `content/event-queue.json` or an enabled official iCalendar feed, and each event page is re-checked live on Monday.
